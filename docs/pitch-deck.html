02 The problem

Every option
is worse than
doing nothing

An illustrator finds their work on a shop page months later. Here is what they can actually do about it today.

OptionWhat happens
Do nothingWhat most people choose. It is the default.
Send an emailIgnored more often than not. No leverage behind it.
DMCA takedownThe image comes down. Nobody gets paid. The use is erased, not licensed.
Recovery agencyThey collect, and keep 30–50% of it. Slow, adversarial, minimum case sizes.
Hire a lawyerCosts more than the licence is worth below roughly four figures.

To verify before quoting: published commission rates for Pixsy, Copytrack, ImageRights and PicRights. The 30–50% band is from their public pages and has not been re-checked for this deck.

03 The gap

The small claim
goes uncollected

A £40 licence is not worth a lawyer, not worth an agency's minimum, and not worth an argument. So it is dropped — and the site owner, who would often have paid £40 up front if anyone had asked, ends up an infringer by accident.

Creator

Wants the money, not a fight

Has a price in mind. Has a folder of screenshots. Has no appetite for confrontation and no budget for one.

Site owner

Wants this to go away cheaply

Usually did not set out to steal anything. Faced with a demand letter they either panic or ignore it.

There are two customers in that problem, not one. Most tools in this space only serve the first.

04 What we built

A settlement rail
both sides
can check

  1. Register. The image, a portfolio URL that proves the wallet is yours, terms in plain words, a base price, and the pages to watch.
  2. Scan. An agent fetches those pages and compares any image it finds with the registered work.
  3. Judge. A leader validator reads the page and proposes a verdict. Other validators repeat the work and vote. Only an agreed verdict is recorded.
  4. Notice. An agreed unlicensed copy becomes a notice with the fee already calculated from the base price, the usage and the prominence.
  5. Settle. The site owner pays and holds an on-chain licence, or disputes with a URL showing permission. A dispute is judged the same way.

Registering, watching and scanning are free. Nobody writes an email at any point.

05 Why GenLayer

The verdict
is the product

One question decides whether this business exists: why would a site owner voluntarily pay a notice generated by a robot?

On our own server

They wouldn't

It is one company's opinion about their page, produced by a model they cannot inspect, with a bill attached. Indistinguishable from a scam — which is exactly why automated copyright notices have a bad name.

On GenLayer

Independent parties agreed

Validators each read the live page, weigh it against terms published before the scan, and vote. The agreed verdict and its reasoning are a transaction anyone can open in the explorer.

Ethereum can hold the money and the terms but cannot read a page or weigh is this the same picture. A centralised AI service can read the page but cannot make its judgement checkable. GenLayer's consensus over non-deterministic work is the only piece that makes an AI verdict something a stranger can rely on — and “a stranger can rely on it” is the whole product.

What is deliberately not claimed: a notice is an automated finding reached by validator consensus, not a legal claim. It has no jurisdiction and no court behind it. Payment is voluntary. What it has is a cheap, checkable, low-conflict path to settling something that would otherwise be dropped.

08 The unknown

One number
decides all of it

Cost per page,
per scan

Validator count × model call × web fetch, on real economics. Nobody knows it, because the product has only ever run where GEN is free.

Why we are saying so

Every projection in this deck is arithmetic on that guess. Measuring it is the first job after this hackathon, and an investor will find it before we do if we do not lead with it.

If it comes back high

Scheduled instead of continuous scanning, many images per page fetch, a cheap first pass with validators only on candidates, and monitoring becomes enterprise while the free tier becomes a one-off scan.

09 Acquisition

The notice
is the
marketing

Every notice is a product demo delivered to the exact person who needs a licence.

  • A creator registers a work — their own self-interest funds the distribution.
  • The agent finds a copy. Validators agree.
  • The notice link reaches the site owner — the buyer side, reached at zero cost.
  • They read a verdict they can check and pay instead of arguing.
  • They now know this exists, and that their other pages are exposed.
  • Next time they license up front. Some of them own work too.
  • The creator gets paid, and tells other creators.

The way in: a free scan

Paste your portfolio URL, get a report of where your images appear. No wallet, no signup. A creator who sees four real copies of their work needs no further persuading — and the screenshot travels.

First channels, in order

The threads where the complaint already happens — r/photography, r/Illustration, ArtStation, illustration Discords — answered by running the free scan in public. Then creator-economy newsletters, then site owners who have already paid one notice, then the GenLayer ecosystem.

10 Search & answer engines

Own the query
nobody wants
to answer

Creators, before they know we exist

“someone used my photo without permission” · “found my art on a website what can I do” · “how much should I charge for image licensing”

Site owners, after a notice lands

“received a copyright notice for an image on my site” · “is this notice legitimate” · “do I have to pay an image licence demand”

The second cluster is the one to win. High intent, almost no competition, and the honest answer — here is what this is, here is what it is not, here is how to check it yourself — is a page nobody selling enforcement wants to write. It does more for trust than any marketing copy.

Already shipped

Pricing and FAQ pages, llms.txt, robots, sitemap, WebSite / SoftwareApplication / FAQPage / Service schema, one canonical origin, per-route metadata, an OG image. Notice pages are noindex on purpose.

Next, in order

Buy the real domain — a vercel.app subdomain will not rank and reads as temporary. Then a licence-fee calculator, an “I received a notice” explainer, and a glossary.

How to be quotable

One source for every fact, stated in full sentences with the number in them. “3% of a licence fee; the creator receives 97%” survives being quoted. “Industry-leading low fees” does not.

11 Roadmap

Measure, then
build the
subscription

Phase 0 · done

Prove the mechanism

The full flow works on chain and a stranger can verify it in five minutes.

Phase 1 · 3 months

Find out what it costs

Measure cost per scan. Ship the free portfolio report. Get 50 creators registering real work. Measure copies found, false positives and settle rate. Buy the domain.

Phase 2–3 · 3–12 months

Licensing, then the rail

Monitoring tiers priced from the measured cost. Buy-a-licence-first. A CMS check before publishing. Then an API other platforms build on.

Kill criterion, stated up front: if fewer than roughly one in ten notices settle, the voluntary-payment premise is wrong and this has to become something else. Better to learn that in month three than in year two. Nothing in Phase 2 starts before the cost number and the settle rate exist.
